[curious] Before we look ten years ahead, I want to share a few ideas of my own. They are research directions, not results. Some of them may be wrong. But I think each one is worth someone's PhD.

[excited] The first idea: rewire tiles, not synapses. Theorem three told us that random sparse graphs starve the GPU. But a tensor core does not see synapses. It sees small dense tiles. So what if structural plasticity worked at exactly that granularity, growing and pruning whole sixteen-by-sixteen blocks, the way RigL grows and prunes individual weights during training? The network still rewires its own topology, but every piece that survives stays dense.

On my laptop, the same ninety percent sparsity ran five and a half times faster as sixteen-by-sixteen tiles than as scattered synapses, even with a naive implementation. A real block-sparse kernel should close much more of the gap.

[curious] The second idea: spectral homeostasis. Theorem one showed that local Hebbian learning collapses toward a single eigenvector. Real neurons fight a similar runaway with homeostatic plasticity. What if the homeostatic target were the shape of the spectrum itself, so that every layer is gently pushed back whenever its effective rank starts to fall, using only statistics it can measure locally? The question I would most like answered is simple: can a purely local rule keep many modes alive, the way attention does?

[excited] The third idea: let the network sleep. Brains separate fast learning from slow consolidation. The hippocampus records the day, and sleep replays it into the cortex. A language model could do the same. Fast weights and delta-rule memories, written during every conversation. Then, offline, replayed and distilled into the slow weights with ordinary backpropagation. Plasticity at inference, stability in training. Two timescales, and perhaps no catastrophic forgetting, if the replay is chosen well.

[curious] And the fourth: three-factor learning for credit. A method called e-prop showed that eligibility traces stored at each synapse, combined with a single broadcast learning signal, can approximate backpropagation through time in spiking networks. Pair that with neuromorphic hardware, and the variance explosion of Theorem three might finally be tamed.

[excited] If any of these sparks something for you, run with it. That is how this field moves forward.
